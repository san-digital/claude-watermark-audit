Sedimentary rock forms when existing rock is broken down by weathering and erosion into fragments, which rivers, wind, ice or gravity then carry to lower ground. These sediments settle in layers on lake beds, floodplains, deltas and the sea floor. As more material accumulates above them, the weight compacts the lower layers and squeezes out much of the trapped water. Minerals dissolved in the water that remains crystallise between the grains and cement them together. Over thousands to millions of years this compaction and cementation, known collectively as lithification, turns loose sediment into solid rock such as sandstone, shale or limestone.
