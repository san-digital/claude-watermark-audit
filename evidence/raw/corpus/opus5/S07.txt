The paper clip emerged in the late nineteenth century, once cheap and springy steel wire became widely available. Samuel Fay patented a bent wire clip in 1867, although he meant it for attaching tickets to fabric rather than for holding paper. The familiar double oval shape, sold as the Gem, was being made in Britain by the 1890s and was never itself patented. Johan Vaaler, a Norwegian, patented a different and far less practical design in 1899. Norway later adopted him as the clip's inventor, a claim that historians of the object firmly dispute.
