A hash collision is when two distinct inputs produce the same digest from a hash function. It is unavoidable in principle: the input space is effectively unbounded and the output space is fixed, so the pigeonhole principle guarantees collisions exist. In a hash table you handle them structurally, either by chaining entries into a bucket or by open addressing with probing, and lookup cost degrades from constant time towards linear as collisions accumulate. For a cryptographic hash, a cheaply constructible collision means the function is broken, which is why MD5 and SHA-1 are no longer acceptable for signatures.
