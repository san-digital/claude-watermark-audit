Sedimentary rock begins with the destruction of older rock. Rain, frost, plant roots and chemical attack break exposed surfaces into loose grains, and moving water, wind or ice carries those grains away. Where the current slows, the material drops out of suspension and builds up in layers, typically on a river basin, a delta or the sea floor. Burial follows. The weight of overlying sediment presses the grains together and forces out pore water, while dissolved minerals precipitate at the grain contacts and glue them into a rigid framework. The result, after long burial, is rock such as sandstone, mudstone or limestone.
