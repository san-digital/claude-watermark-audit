Right, one year in. Thought I'd write up how it's actually gone, because everything I read beforehand was either "bees are basically self-sufficient, just leave them alone" or "you will fail and it will be your fault", and the truth sits somewhere awkward in the middle.

Started with one nuc in April, which everyone tells you is a mistake because you've got nothing to compare it against. They were right. When my colony went a bit odd in June I had absolutely no idea whether that was normal odd or emergency odd. Bought a second colony in July purely as a control group, which I appreciate is a slightly unhinged reason to buy livestock.

Things nobody warned me about, in no particular order. One: the smoker. Everybody demonstrates the smoker with a serene expression and a beautiful cool white plume. Mine goes out. Always. I have lit that thing four times in a single inspection while a colony gradually decided it had opinions about me. I now carry a blowtorch, which feels like cheating and is absolutely staying.

Two: you will get stung and it genuinely is fine, right up until you get stung on the back of the hand and spend three days looking like you've smuggled a bread roll under your skin. The wrist gap between glove and sleeve is a design flaw in every suit ever made.

Three: swarming. Mine went in late May. I saw the queen cells the week before and thought, hmm, interesting, I'll read about that later. Later arrived as a roaring cloud of my own bees settling in next door's plum tree, where they stayed for six hours while I stood underneath holding a cardboard box and my dignity. Got most of them back. Neighbour has been remarkably decent about the whole thing and now gets honey for life.

Four: the honey. I took about eleven kilos off the two colonies, which is apparently modest, and I do not care in the slightest because it is the best thing I have ever eaten and I made none of it. There is something faintly ridiculous about accepting compliments for work done entirely by insects.

What I'd tell past me: get two colonies from the start, join the local association even if you find group activities exhausting, and do the varroa treatment on the calendar rather than when you get round to it. Also, buy the good hive tool. The cheap one bends.

Anyway. Year two starts in about six weeks and I'm already anxious about it. Ask me anything.
