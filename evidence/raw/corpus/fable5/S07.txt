The paper clip took its modern form in the late nineteenth century, when cheap steel wire and new bending machinery made mass production practical. The Gem clip, the double-loop design still standard today, appeared in Britain in the 1890s and was never patented in that exact form. The Norwegian inventor Johan Vaaler patented a similar clip in 1899, and during the Second World War Norwegians wore paper clips as a quiet symbol of resistance to occupation. Cheap, reusable, and requiring no explanation, the design has barely changed in more than a century of daily use.
