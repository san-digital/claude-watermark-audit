Sedimentary rock begins as loose material: sand, silt, clay, shell fragments, and the remains of plants and animals. Rivers, wind, glaciers, and ocean currents carry this sediment until it settles, layer upon layer, in basins, deltas, and on the sea floor. As the layers pile up, the weight of the overburden compacts the grains below and forces out the water trapped between them. Mineral-rich groundwater then deposits calcite, quartz, or iron compounds in the remaining pore spaces, cementing the grains into solid rock. This combination of compaction and cementation, called lithification, produces sandstone, mudstone, and limestone over vast stretches of time.
