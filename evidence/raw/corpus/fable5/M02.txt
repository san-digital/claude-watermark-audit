So I've just hit the one year mark of keeping bees and figured I'd write up the highlights for anyone thinking about starting, because a year ago I was reading posts exactly like this one and nodding along without understanding half of it.

First confession: I bought all the wrong stuff. Everyone does, apparently. I got the fancy jacket with the fencing veil (fine), a stainless smoker the size of a fire extinguisher (overkill), and about four gadgets whose names I've forgotten and whose purpose I never discovered. What I actually use every week is the hive tool, the smoker, and a bit of patience. That's the kit list. Save your money.

The bees arrived in May as a nucleus colony, five frames of brood and a marked queen, and I drove them home at 20 mph with the windows down like I was transporting nitroglycerine. Installing them was the single most nerve-racking thing I've done in years, and it went completely fine, which turned out to be the pattern for the whole season. The anticipation is always worse than the inspection.

Things nobody told me: bees have moods. Genuinely. On warm, calm days with a good nectar flow, you can practically rummage through the hive while they ignore you. On muggy days before a storm they will let you know, personally and repeatedly, that you are not welcome. I learned to check the forecast before inspecting the same way I used to check it before a barbecue.

I got stung eight times over the year, seven of them my own fault (bare ankles, rushing, once memorably trapping a bee in my sleeve like an idiot). It hurts less than you fear and more than beekeepers admit.

The low point was late August, when I discovered wasps hammering the entrance and a colony that felt suddenly quiet. Reduced the entrance, fed them syrup, crossed everything. The high point, weirdly, wasn't the honey. It was mid-June, opening the hive and finally spotting eggs, tiny white commas at the bottom of the cells, and realising I could actually see them now. My eyes hadn't changed. I'd just learned what to look for.

Oh, the honey. Twelve jars from one hive in its first year, which the books say you shouldn't expect and which I'm therefore taking full credit for. It tastes like blackberry and something I can't name, and I have given jars to basically everyone I've ever met.

Would I recommend it? Absolutely, with the caveat that "keeping" bees is generous. They keep themselves. You just show up every week, try not to squash the queen, and take the blame or the credit depending on how it goes. Ask me anything, I'm now a veteran of exactly one year and therefore dangerously confident.
