Sedimentary rock forms when fragments of older rock, mineral grains, and organic material are transported by water, wind, or ice and settle in layers, usually on the floors of rivers, lakes, and seas. As sediment accumulates, the weight of newer deposits compacts the layers beneath, squeezing out water between the grains. Dissolved minerals such as calcite, silica, and iron oxides then precipitate in the remaining pore spaces and cement the grains together, a process called lithification. The result is layered rock such as sandstone, shale, and limestone, which often preserves fossils and a record of the environment in which the sediment was laid down.
