Sedimentary rock forms through the accumulation and compaction of sediments over millions of years. Weathering and erosion break down existing rocks into small fragments of sand, silt, and clay. These particles are transported by water, wind, or ice and deposited in layers at the bottom of oceans, rivers, or lakes. As successive layers pile up, the weight of overlying material compresses the lower sediments. Over time, minerals in groundwater cement the particles together in a process called lithification, transforming loose sediment into solid rock. This process explains why sedimentary rocks often contain fossils and visible layering.
