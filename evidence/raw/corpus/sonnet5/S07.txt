The paper clip as we know it took shape in the late 1800s, once wire bending machines made small metal fasteners cheap to produce. Norwegian inventor Johan Vaaler patented a clip design in 1899, though similar shapes had already appeared elsewhere in Europe. The now familiar double oval "Gem" clip was never formally patented, and its true inventor remains unclear to this day. By the early 1900s, offices worldwide had adopted it for holding loose papers together without punching holes through them. Its simple spring steel design has barely changed in over a century, making it one of the most enduring small inventions still in everyday use.
