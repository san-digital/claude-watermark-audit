Sedimentary rock begins as loose fragments of sand, silt, clay, or shell debris carried by water, wind, or ice and deposited in layers, often on a seabed, lakebed, or riverbed. As layers accumulate, the weight of material above compacts the lower layers, forcing out water and pressing the grains together. Minerals dissolved in groundwater, commonly silica or calcium carbonate, then crystallize in the remaining pore spaces and bind the grains into solid rock, a process called cementation. Over long spans of time this compaction and cementation, together known as lithification, produce familiar rocks such as sandstone, limestone, and shale.
