A hash collision happens when two different inputs produce the same output from a hash function. Since hash functions map an effectively unbounded input space onto a fixed-size output space, collisions are mathematically unavoidable, though a good function makes them rare and unpredictable. In hash tables, collisions mean two keys land in the same bucket, so you need a resolution strategy like chaining or open addressing. In cryptographic contexts, collisions are more serious, since an attacker who can deliberately produce two inputs with the same hash can forge signatures or tamper with data undetected.
