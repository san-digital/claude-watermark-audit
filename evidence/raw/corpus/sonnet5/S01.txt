Sedimentary rock forms when particles of sand, silt, clay, or organic debris settle out of water, ice, or wind and accumulate in layers. As more material piles on top, the growing weight compresses the lower layers, squeezing out water and pressing grains together. Groundwater carrying dissolved minerals, often calcite or silica, seeps through the remaining gaps and precipitates, cementing the grains into solid rock, a process called lithification. This usually happens over thousands or millions of years in riverbeds, deltas, lake bottoms, and ocean floors, producing rocks such as sandstone, shale, and limestone, which frequently preserve fossils and layering.
