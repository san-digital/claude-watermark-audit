Okay so it's officially been a year since I talked my partner into letting me put a beehive in the back corner of the garden, and I have THOUGHTS. Nobody tells you how much of beekeeping is just standing there in a slightly too-hot suit, sweating, staring at frames like you understand what you're looking at. Spoiler: for the first three months I absolutely did not.

I started with a single nucleus colony in April, five frames of bees and a queen I named Beyoncé because I figured if I was going to be nervous around fifty thousand insects I might as well be nervous around royalty. The first inspection was a disaster. I dropped a frame (thankfully into the hive, not onto the grass), got stung twice through my glove because apparently gardening gloves are not the flex I thought they were, and spent the rest of the day convinced I'd doomed the whole colony. They were fine. They did not care about my feelings at all, which honestly set the tone for the whole year.

Summer was the good bit. Watching them come back loaded with pollen, orange and yellow and this weird grey-blue from something in next door's garden, never stopped being satisfying. I got genuinely obsessed with figuring out what they were foraging on. There's a real detective-work side to this that nobody warns you about. By July the colony had built up enough that I added a second box, and by August I actually got a small harvest, about nine kilos, which felt like an insane amount of effort for what amounted to a few jars of honey, and also somehow completely worth it.

Autumn brought the varroa mite panic, which every beekeeping forum on earth had already warned me about and I still underestimated. Treated late, worried a lot, learned more about mite counts than I ever expected to know about anything. Winter was mostly anxious silence, tapping the side of the hive on mild days just to hear that reassuring hum and prove to myself they hadn't all quietly died while I wasn't looking.

They made it through. The cluster's smaller than I'd like going into spring, but they're alive, Beyoncé is apparently still queening, and I've already ordered a second hive for this year because apparently one wasn't enough chaos. Ask me again in twelve months whether that was a good idea.
